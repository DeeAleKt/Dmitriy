## 1. Which ergonomic models are sold in Ukraine at ≤6,000 UAH now? (brand-by-brand coverage)

### Takeaway
Seat sliders are almost absent from the ≤6,000 UAH segment of Ukrainian retail. The brands known for sliders and adjustable lumbar support (Sihoo, Barsky ECO Slider, Kreslalux, Richman's Ergo line, SpineLine, Hbada, Mealux) sell for about 7,000–40,000 UAH in Ukraine. Within budget there are mostly "basic ergonomic" mesh chairs, with a synchro or tilt mechanism, a headrest, and sometimes height-adjustable lumbar and 3D armrests. The best-documented in-budget candidates are **OfficePro Atlas OC630** (about 5,900–6,200 UAH), **GT Racer B-530B** (about 6,140–6,600 UAH, at the 6,500 stretch cap) and **HATTA Light Mesh** (about 5,400–6,000 UAH). The rest are simpler mesh chairs at roughly 1,400–5,600 UAH.

### Inferences
- In Ukraine the ergonomic "value" brands (Sihoo, Hbada) are sold at prices well above their US sale prices (US$169.99 for M18 and M102C). Within 6,000 UAH you get OEM-style mesh chairs from local or house brands (OfficePro, GT Racer, HATTA, Special4You, AMF, Nowy Styl, Richman's budget line, Bonro), not the Sihoo-class designs.
- "Ergonomic" in the ≤6,000 UAH segment usually means a mesh back, a synchro or tilt mechanism, a headrest and sometimes height-adjustable lumbar. A real seat slider (seat-depth adjustment), the most useful feature for a shared adult/child chair, was **not confirmed** on any in-budget model. The nearest confirmed slider chairs are GT Racer B-562 (6,940–7,599 UAH), Mealux D1 Air (8,900 UAH) and GT Racer B-2450 (9,180–9,999 UAH).
- If the household can go to about 7,000–8,600 UAH, a Sihoo M76 colour variant (7,000 UAH when in stock) or the Sihoo M101C (8,550 UAH) becomes reachable. These are outside the brief's cap.

## 2. Adjustability per model (seat depth, lumbar, headrest, heights, armrests, mechanism, load, base, gas lift, material, warranty)

### Takeaway
Of the in-budget chairs, only **OfficePro Atlas OC630** has a reasonably complete spec sheet:
- seat height 46–53 cm
- 3D armrests at 65–79 cm from the floor
- height-adjustable lumbar cushion
- headrest (non-removable)
- synchro mechanism with a 3-position lock, reclining to 113°
- class-4 gas lift, nylon base
- 12-month warranty
- rated 120 kg (one shop says 100–150 kg)

Its seat-depth adjustment is claimed by only one shop. **GT Racer B-530B** has a 50×50 cm seat, 47–54 cm seat height, a height-adjustable headrest, synchro and a higher load rating (135–160 kg), but it weighs 26 kg and its seat-depth adjustment is unclear. ## 5. Suitability for a 9-year-old (~130–140 cm, 28–35 kg) sharing the chair

### Takeaway
None of the in-budget chairs is built for a child.
- **Seat height**: minimums are 46–47 cm (Atlas 46, B-530B 47, AMF Matrix HR 46).

- **Armrests**: Atlas's lowest setting is about 19 cm above the seat (estimate).

Without a seat slider, a **footrest plus a firm back cushion** (to shorten the effective seat depth) would be needed for the child. These rest on general anthropometric rules of thumb that were **not sourced in this research**; cross-check them.
- **Feet will not reach the floor.** A 130–140 cm child's popliteal height is roughly mid-30s cm. At 46–47 cm minimum seat height the feet would hang about 8–12 cm above the floor, so a footrest (about 10–15 cm high, ideally adjustable) is needed for multi-hour sessions.
- **Seat is too deep.** A 9-year-old's buttock-to-knee-pit length is roughly high-30s cm, against 48–52 cm fixed seats. Sitting back would press the seat edge into the back of the knees, so the child will slide forward and sit unsupported. A firm wedge or back cushion about 8–12 cm thick (or a chair with a slider whose shortest setting is about 42–44 cm or less) is needed for the backrest and lumbar to touch the child's back at all.
- **Lumbar position.** Once a cushion brings the child's back into contact, an adult lumbar pad, even at its lowest setting, will probably sit at the child's mid-back or lower shoulder-blade area rather than the lower back. Atlas's height-adjustable lumbar, lowered fully, is the best of the in-budget options here. Range in cm unknown.
- **Armrests.** Atlas's lowest armrest point is about 19 cm above the seat (estimate). That is above a typical 9-year-old's seated elbow height of roughly mid-to-high teens in cm, so the child should not lean on the armrests (shoulder hunching). For the 185 cm adult, about 19–26 cm above the seat fits well.
- **Daily re-adjustment.** Sharing "roughly equally" means changing seat height (and lumbar, armrests and headrest) at every handover. A class-4 gas lift (Atlas) and simple levers help. A seat slider would remove the need for a cushion for the child.

### Gaps
- No model's **shortest effective seat depth** (slider minimum) was found within budget.
- No lumbar height ranges in cm were found for any in-budget chair.
- Anthropometric data for 9-year-olds should come from the children's-chair researcher's notes or a standard such as ISO/EN 1729 or DSTU tables; they were not retrieved here.
