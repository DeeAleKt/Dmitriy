Every price and spec below comes from search-engine summaries of Ukrainian retailer and brand pages, retrieved on **16 Oct 2026**. ## 1. Which "growing"/teen ergonomic chairs are sold in Ukraine, and what are their specs and prices?

### Takeaway
Ukrainian stores carry many growing/teen chair brands: Mealux and its budget sub-brand Evo-kids, FunDesk, Cubby, Duorest, SingBee, GT Racer Kids, and a budget "Champion GTS". Almost all are rated for 60–90 kg, and a few Mealux models claim 100–110 kg. The only kids' chair found with a load rating well above 120 kg is the SingBee 142L (160 kg), and it costs about 14,550–16,750 UAH. I found no Ukrainian listings for Comf-Pro kids' chairs, Libao or Kidsmill.

## 2. Can any of them credibly carry a 185 cm, 80–100 kg adult?

### Takeaway
No. I found no chair under 6,000 UAH that suits a 9-year-old and also credibly carries a 185 cm, 80–100 kg adult. The details:
- Kids' and growing chairs are rated 60–110 kg and have child-sized seats and backrests. For example, the Duo Kid has a 36 cm backrest and 32–38 cm seat depth.
- The only kids' chair rated 160 kg costs about 15,000 UAH.
- Mealux's 120 kg "teen/adult" chairs start at age 15 and cost about 10,000–13,000 UAH.

I also found no real-user evidence of adults using these chairs.

### Inferences
- On load alone, no in-budget kids' or growing chair qualifies. The brief asks for at least 120 kg with margin, and the best in-budget models stop at 100–110 kg, with some of those figures disputed between stores.
- The geometry also rules them out:
  - A 36 cm backrest and a 32–38 cm seat depth suit roughly a 120–160 cm child.
  - A 185 cm adult typically needs about 45–50 cm of seat depth and a backrest that reaches at least the shoulder blades. These adult figures are rules of thumb I did not source here; the ergonomics researcher should confirm them.
- GT Racer's own spec for the X-1414 Kids (maximum 170 cm) rules out a 185 cm user.
- Mealux's teen/adult chairs (Mentor Duo, from 15) solve the adult side, but they are not marketed for a 9-year-old and cost about 1.7 times the budget.
- **Verdict for the report:** within 6,000 UAH there is no true "family" growing chair that genuinely fits both users.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve anthropometric data for child or adult seat dimensions; the ergonomics researcher covers this.
- I found no reviews, YouTube tests or forum posts from adults using kids' growing chairs.
- I did not capture the seat and backrest dimensions of the SingBee 142L, which are needed to judge whether an adult would fit.

## 3. Kids' versions of gaming chairs: what exists, and are they any good?

### Takeaway
GT Racer is the only brand I found selling dedicated "Kids" gaming chairs in Ukraine (X-1414 Kids and X-5934-B Kids, about 2,000–4,000 UAH). What the accessories fix:
- The footrest stops the child's feet dangling once the seat is raised to desk height.
- A thick lumbar cushion moves the child forward, so the seat edge no longer presses behind the knees.

What they do not fix:
- armrests that are too wide or high
- the side bolsters of a bucket-style gaming seat
- a lumbar zone that sits too high for a child's back

Practical caveats:
- The adult-side chair still needs a rating of at least 120 kg with margin. A 100 kg rating is not "enough" just because the child is light.
- Every bundle has to be re-adjusted at each swap (seat height, cushion in or out). A gas lift makes the height change quick, and the cushions are easy to remove.

## 6. Brief: what orthopedists and ergonomists (including Ukrainian sources) say about kids in adult chairs versus dedicated kids' chairs

### Takeaway
The Ukrainian sources I found are mostly retailer and blog guides, not doctors. They agree that in a large adult seat a child either dangles their feet or perches on the front edge with no back support, which leads to slouching. They recommend:
- feet flat on the floor or on a footrest, with knees at about 90°
- a seat edge that does not press behind the knees
- lumbar support
- a 5-star base
- no armrests, or fixed ones

I did not retrieve any primary source from a pediatric orthopedist; the ergonomics researcher covers this in depth.

### Cited Findings
- In an adult office chair with a large seat, a child either cannot reach the floor or has to sit on the front edge, losing back support. The result is slouching and a curved spine. The same snippet says the spine keeps forming until about age 20–22 and recommends adjustable chairs for children who sit 5 or more hours. It came from a cluster of Ukrainian retailer blogs, and I could not confirm which exact page — [Economtochka](https://economtochka.com.ua/ua/ergonomichnoe-kompjuternoe-kreslo-vybiraem-udobnyj-stul-dlja-rebenka), [Daddy-store](https://daddy-store.com.ua/osoblyvosti-kompiuternykh-krisel-dlia-ditei-na-shcho-zvernuty-uvahu/)
- A Ukrainian physical-education journal says children's furniture should match the child's height — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/8d9706fed1ca4722bc9cb0df7034ad53)
- Retailer guides from taburetka.ua, svetum.com.ua, comfort-pro.com.ua and renews.com.ua make these recommendations:
  - The child's feet should rest fully on the floor with knees at about 90°, and the seat edge should not press under the knees.
  - A 5-star base is the most stable, and the backrest should support the lumbar curve.
  - Some say armrests are unnecessary because leaning on them makes the child slouch.
  - A growing chair lasts about 3–5 years.
  - Test the chair with the child before buying, and see a doctor if the child already has spine problems.
  - Sources: [Taburetka](https://www.taburetka.ua/statti/vybyraemo-stilec-dlya-shkolyara-instrukciya-dlya-profilaktyky-skoliozu-753), [Svetum](https://svetum.com.ua/ua/blog/sovety-pokupatelyam/vybrat-ortopedicheskoe-kreslo-dlya-shkolnika/), [Comfort-pro](https://comfort-pro.com.ua/yak-pravylno-vybraty-krislo-dlia-dytyny-shkoliara/), [reNews](https://renews.com.ua/oglyady/iak-pidibraty-ortopedychne-krislo-dlia-shkoliara-za-vikom-zrostom-i-vahoiu/)
- GT Racer, which sells these chairs, says pediatricians advise either no armrests or fixed ones for children — [gtracer.com.ua](https://gtracer.com.ua/should-i-buy-a-kids-gaming-chair)
- The Epicentr guide puts typical growing-chair load limits at 60–90 kg. It is a buying guide, not medical advice — [Epicentr article](https://epicentrk.ua/ua/articles/kak-vybrat-shkolnoe-kreslo-v-detskuyu-lang-yak-vibrati-shkilne-krislo-v-dityachu.html)

### Inferences
- Both paths can satisfy the shared rules: feet supported, knees at about 90°, a seat short enough for the child to reach the backrest, and lumbar support at the child's height. A dedicated growing chair meets them by design. An adult chair with a footrest and a back cushion meets them only roughly, and only if it is re-adjusted at every swap.
- For a child sitting several hours a day, that makes path (b), two chairs, ergonomically safer for the child. Path (a), one chair, is better value for the adult.
