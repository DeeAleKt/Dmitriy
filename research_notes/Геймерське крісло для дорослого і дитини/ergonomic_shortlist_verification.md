### Inferences
- **Armrest heights look wrong.** The "38 cm від сидіння" figure (B-522-1, B-532) and Epicentr's 310–380 mm are implausible as pad height above the seat surface for a usable office chair; typical designs sit about 18–28 cm above the seat. They are probably measured from another reference, such as the seat plate or the floor at the lowest gas-lift setting. Both users should test armrest height in person. This "typical" figure is a general rule of thumb, not sourced here.
- **Seat height for the child.** If the shared desk is a standard fixed 72–76 cm adult desk, the 9-year-old will need the seat **near its maximum** for the desk to be near elbow height, plus a tall footrest. A higher maximum seat height therefore helps the child at an adult desk, and the minimum matters little. The minimum (B-522-1/B-532 42 cm; HATTA Flex 40 cm) matters only with a low or height-adjustable desk. This rests on a rule of thumb (a child's seated elbow height is about 13–14% of stature, i.e. about 18 cm at 135 cm) that was not sourced here; cross-check it against the children's-chair notes.
- **Seat depth for the child.** Even a slider will probably not bring a 49–50 cm seat down to a 9-year-old's thigh length; typical sliders travel about 5 cm (unsourced rule of thumb). A slider reduces the back-cushion thickness the child needs but probably does not eliminate it. The fixed 53 cm seat of the 2E OC150 is the worst for the child. The 46 cm seat of the Richman Blast is the shallowest of the in-budget chairs.
- **Load rating.** For a 100 kg adult, the 150 kg ratings of B-530B and B-532 give comfortable headroom. Atlas, 2E, Richman and AMF are rated at 120 kg. B-522-1's rating is unknown and must be checked before buying.

## 4. Does any ≤6,000 UAH chair combine a seat slider, adjustable lumbar and a headrest that reaches a 185 cm adult?

### Takeaway
**No chair was confirmed with all three.**
- **GT Racer B-522-1** has the slider (spec table) and a height- and tilt-adjustable mesh headrest. Its lumbar support is built into the double-mesh back, with no adjustment mentioned, and its headrest reach is unknown.
- **Atlas** had the best paper combination: shop-claimed slider, height-adjustable lumbar, adjustable headrest. It is now probably over budget, and its estimated headrest top of about 66–76 cm above the seat is likely to land at the neck of a 185 cm adult, not the back of the head.
- **Published anthropometric percentiles** put the back of a 185 cm man's head about 80–88 cm above the seat. That estimate is derived and explained below.

### Cited Findings
- **Sitting height (seat to crown), 95th percentile men:**
  - British (19–65) 965 mm; US 975 mm; Hong Kong 955 mm; Japan 950 mm. — [CityU anthropometry, sitting height](https://personal.cityu.edu.hk/meachan/online%20anthropometry/Chapter2/Ch2-8.htm)
  - Continental's spec: Germany 965 mm; USA 985 mm; China 958 mm. — [Continental anthropometric data](https://cdn.continental.com/fileadmin/user_upload/demo_content/images/anthropometric_data_en_combined.pdf)
- **Seated shoulder height (seat to acromion), 95th percentile men:**
  - British 645 mm; US 655 mm; Hong Kong 655 mm; Japan 635 mm. — [CityU anthropometry, sitting shoulder height](https://personal.cityu.edu.hk/meachan/online%20anthropometry/Chapter2/Ch2-10.htm)
  - NCSU's summary table gives 25.71 in, about 65.3 cm. — [NCSU anthropometric tables](https://www.ergocenter.ncsu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/18/2017/09/Anthropometric-Summary-Data-Tables.pdf)
- **Seated eye height:** NCSU lists 33.86 for the 95th-percentile male. The units are not shown in the snippet; inches (about 86 cm) is the likely reading. — [NCSU anthropometric tables](https://www.ergocenter.ncsu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/18/2017/09/Anthropometric-Summary-Data-Tables.pdf); [NCSU 2026 summary tables](https://ergocenter.ncsu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/358/2026/02/01-Anthropometry-Summary-Tables.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Proxy.** A 185 cm man sits around the upper (about 90th–95th) percentile of British, US and German men's stature. That ranking is general knowledge, not sourced here. The 95th-percentile values above are therefore used as a proxy for the adult.
- **Head and neck heights for a 185 cm adult.**
  - Crown of the head: about 96–98 cm above the seat.
  - Eyes: about 86 cm.
  - Shoulders: about 64–66 cm.
  - The occiput (the back-of-head contact zone for a headrest) is therefore roughly 80–88 cm above the seat.
  - The neck spans roughly 66–80 cm.
  - These are estimates; seat compression and recline shift them by a few cm.
- **Atlas and B-530B headrests.**
  - Method: overall height minus seat height at both ends of the gas-lift range.
  - Atlas's headrest top is about 66–76 cm above the seat; B-530B's about 69–75 cm.
  - **Both stop at or below the neck of a 185 cm adult.** They would work as neck pillows, not head supports. This matches the generic tall-user complaint in Q5 that standard headrests land "at the neck or shoulders".
- **Backrest height.** Per Pheasant's 60–80% rule, the 185 cm adult needs only about 39–52 cm of backrest for upright work. Every in-budget chair here (58–70 cm) meets that. The headrest, not the backrest, is the limiting part for the tall adult.
- **Best headrest reach.** Over the cap, B-562's 75 cm back plus a dual-adjust headrest is the most likely to reach the 80–88 cm zone. This is unverified because no total height was found.

### Gaps
- No in-budget chair publishes headrest travel or the height of the headrest top above the seat. B-522-1, B-532, BM-01 and 2E OC150 cannot be assessed for headrest reach without an in-store measurement.
- Child anthropometrics (popliteal height, buttock-to-knee-crease length and seated elbow height at age 9) were not retrieved; one search found only study titles, not values. The EN 1729-1 size-3/4 seat dimensions were not retrieved. See the children's-chair researcher's notes.
